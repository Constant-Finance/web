View Points
Three things at once

These three rarely coexist.

The rate is fixed

Priced at signing. Whatever the market does afterwards has nothing to do with this loan.

Repay whenever you want

A fixed rate usually means being locked in. Not here. What you signed is a ceiling, not a sentence.

Nothing deposited up front

Funds are drawn the moment a match settles, and until then they sit in your own wallet.

How to start

Four steps. One transaction.

01

Sign an order

Borrowers sign a borrow order, suppliers sign a supply order. Off-chain, no gas.

02

Get matched

Compatible terms clear. One loan can be funded by several supply orders together.

03

Settle

Transfers, collateral, position creation and claim minting all happen in one transaction.

04

Repay

Any time. Payments cover interest first, then principal.

Who this is for

Built for both sides of a loan.

Lenders

Your capital keeps earning where it is, until a higher rate is matched here. You set the minimum rate. Nothing fills below it.

Yield strategy runners

Arbitrage, LP, staking. The margin is 1–2%, and a floating rate can erase it mid-trade. With the cost capped at signing, the math works before you commit.

Event-driven borrowing

The demand is real, but the decision stalls on what rates will do next. Here it collapses to one question: does it pencil out at signing? If yes, sign.

Long-horizon collateralized

Borrowing against assets you'd rather hold than sell. When the cost has no ceiling, the position never gets opened. A capped cost lets you borrow now, even if you expect the collateral to rise.
